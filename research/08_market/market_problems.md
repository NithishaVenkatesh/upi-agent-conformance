## How these are ranked

Ranked on four axes, in this order of weight:

1. **Evidence strength** — can the headline number be traced to a primary or near-primary source I actually opened? A problem with a great story and a vendor blog behind it ranks *below* a duller problem with a regulator behind it. **A fabricated or unverifiable statistic in a pitch is fatal, so evidence quality is weighted first, not last.**
2. **Cost / size of pain.**
3. **India-specificity** — the target is Razorpay. A problem that only exists in India is worth more than a generic one.
4. **Demonstrability** — can this be shown convincingly in a hackathon demo with synthetic or test-mode data?

### Evidence-grade key
| Grade | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **A** | Regulator / card network / industry body primary document, fetched and read. |
| **B** | Credible secondary reporting a primary, or platform telemetry with a disclosed base. |
| **C** | Vendor-published with methodology, or analyst house, unverified fetch. |
| **D** | ⚠️ Vendor marketing, no methodology, sources mutually inconsistent. **Do not put on a slide.** |
| **—** | `EVIDENCE NOT FOUND`. Mechanism may be real; magnitude is unknown. |

## Top 3 recommendations, with reasoning

**If evidence quality is what wins:** **#1 (UPI business decline)** and **#2 (MSME receivables)**. Both are India-only, both have a number traceable to NPCI or a named tracked report, and neither requires defending a vendor claim. #1 has the sharper hook — *"every single bank NPCI publishes data for is failing NPCI's own target, and it's not a technology problem"* — and maps to **Track 3**.

**If demonstrability is what wins:** **#6 (multi-source reconciliation)**. It is the only problem in the pack where **the demo is stronger than any statistic**: put four files on screen that all describe the same rupee and cannot be joined. Every finance person in the room has lived it. Fully reproducible in test mode. **Track 4.** Its weakness — no headline number — stops mattering if you don't try to use one.

**If the track's own scoring rubric is what wins:** **#3 (RTO)** or **#5 (fraud)** for **Track 2**, which explicitly requires precision/recall on a held-out test set. #3 is better: binary label, known outcome for every historical order, explicit asymmetric cost matrix (a false positive suppresses a real sale; a false negative costs two shipping legs plus blocked inventory), and it is a problem that essentially only exists in India. **Its numbers are the weakest thing about it** — pitch the mechanism and the Unicommerce 150m-transaction trend, not a vendor RTO percentage.
