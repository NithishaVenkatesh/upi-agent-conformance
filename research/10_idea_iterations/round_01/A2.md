# A2 · Merchant Attestation Service — filling the empty `keys[]` slot

**Judged:** 2026-08-26 · **Panel:** IdeaAgent · **Round:** 01 · **Track:** 01

---

## One-line pitch
Mint Razorpay's existing KYC + dispute-rate trust verdict as a signed, agent-queryable claim into the `keys[]` slot UCP already defines and that 0 of 4 live Indian merchants populate.

## Problem
Everything in the field verifies **the agent** — Visa TAP, Mastercard Agent Pay, Forter, ACP, AP2. Nothing verifies **the merchant to the agent**, and ACP forbids it by design (`MUST NOT` return `merchant_id`). Razorpay already computes that verdict and renders it as an icon a human clicks: *"You can validate that a business is a Razorpay Trusted Business by clicking on the RTB icon."* A clickable badge is useless to an agent.

## User
Buyer agents deciding whether a discovered merchant is safe to transact with; merchants who want agent traffic to convert.

## Solution
An attestation issuer consuming merchant signals (KYC status, dispute rate, settlement history, chargeback ratio, account age), producing a bounded, expiring, revocable signed claim published into `keys[]`, plus a verifier an agent runs before committing money — with revocation and freshness semantics.

## AI role
LLM adjudicates unstructured merchant signals a rules engine cannot join — policy-page text, refund-policy plausibility, catalogue/legal-entity consistency — producing a graded rationale. Deliberately not AI: the dispute-rate threshold, the KYC boolean, signature generation/verification, expiry checks, and the final issue/deny gate. AI produces evidence; a rule produces the verdict.

## Razorpay role
The single most "only-a-PSP-could-do-this" framing in the pool. Razorpay is already the authority on a merchant's KYC and dispute rate; the idea is "publish what you already know, in a format a machine can consume."

## Competitors
None found on the merchant→agent direction. The Track 01 research agent initially called merchant trust "the most crowded space of all" and then overturned itself — the crowded direction is agent→merchant.

## Judge reaction

**First 10 seconds.** "Everyone's verifying the agent — nobody verifies the merchant." Good line. Judges nod. It is a reframe they have not heard today.

**First 60 seconds.** Problem clear, user clear, solution clear. Why-now is the weakest of the four: the honest answer is "before agents get scammed", which is a prediction. The `keys[]`-is-empty-on-all-four fact does real work here and should be led with.

**After demo.** Remembered: *"the RTB badge is a picture, and agents can't see pictures."* That is the whole memory, and it is a good one. Not remembered: any number.

**Deliberation.**

> **Judge A (product):** I like the direction. Nobody's covering it. But who buys this? Razorpay would build it in-house in a sprint, and they're the only credible issuer.
> **Judge C (hackathon):** That's true of half the good infrastructure ideas. It doesn't disqualify.
> **Judge B (senior eng):** My problem is upstream of all that. He has a hundred merchants with planted risk labels. Where did the labels come from?
> **Judge D:** He wrote them.
> **Judge B:** He wrote the merchants, he wrote their dispute rates, he wrote their refund policies, and he wrote which ones are bad. Then he reports precision and recall on recovering his own assignment. There is no world in which that number is informative. It measures whether his scorer implements his generator.
> **Judge A:** He says the labels are planted *independently of the features* to defeat leakage.
> **Judge B:** Then the task is unlearnable and the number should be chance. If it isn't chance, the independence claim is false. Either way the table is dead. You cannot escape that box by declaring independence — you escape it by getting labels from somewhere you didn't write.
> **Judge D (AI judgment):** And the model reads invented refund policies to judge the plausibility of invented merchants. That is a model grading fiction.
> **Judge E (chair):** The direction is right and the evidence is unobtainable. Those are different problems and only one of them is fixable in ten days.

**Does not survive deliberation.** The objection is not about execution quality; it is about whether the central claim is checkable at all.

## Strongest advantage
The cleanest "only a PSP could ship this" story of the six, anchored on a shipped Razorpay product (RTB) and a verified empty slot (`keys[]` unpopulated on 4/4 live merchants). The inversion of the field's direction of travel is real and was verified rather than assumed — including a recorded reversal of the researcher's own earlier conclusion, which is itself credible.

## Strongest weakness
The system's output is a *judgement about the world* — "this merchant is safe" — and the builder has no access to the world. Every other idea in the pool can at least anchor on an external document, a live URL, or an executable payment. This one anchors on nothing.

## Fatal risk

> **The strongest reason this idea loses: merchant trustworthiness has no obtainable ground truth, so the headline number is unfalsifiable by construction — and the panel will find this in one question.**

The corpus is explicit that the single strongest move in the field is "validate on something you did not generate." A2 is the one idea of the six for which that move is *structurally impossible*. There is no public dataset of Indian merchants labelled by whether they defrauded an AI agent, because no AI agent has yet been defrauded by one. The builder must invent the merchants, invent their signals, and invent the truth — and then report precision and recall against his own invention. That is the field's defining failure with better cryptography.

The stated defence ("labels planted independently of the features") is worse than the disease, and Judge B's dilemma is airtight: independent labels make the task unlearnable; a good score therefore proves the labels were not independent. There is no exit.

**Second-order risk (pillar 3).** The design concedes the LLM is advisory and the rule is decisive. An honest ablation — the corpus's most-recommended artifact — will almost certainly show that deleting the LLM leaves the verdict unchanged. The builder then either publishes a row that says the AI does nothing, or omits the ablation and loses the honesty edge. Both outcomes are bad.

**Third risk.** Issuing trust verdicts about named real businesses, even in a demo, is a reputational and legal hazard the builder does not need.

## Competitor saturation
**LOW** in the merchant→agent direction (verified). **HIGH** in the adjacent verifiable-credentials / attestation genre generally, where the plumbing is commodity and the judges have seen it.

---

## Verdict: **NO-GO**

**Reason: the evidence problem is not a build problem and cannot be fixed by iteration.** The idea's core claim is a judgement about real merchants; the builder has no access to real merchants; therefore the submission's headline number is invented at both ends. Ten days will not change that. Every other idea in this pool has at least one external anchor — A1 has live URLs, A4 has primary regulatory documents, B11 could have real catalogues. A2 has none.

**It is not, however, worthless — it is a component.** The verifier and the `keys[]` publication step are a strong 40-line addition to A1, where they inherit A1's external anchor (the `keys[]` slot is verifiably empty on four real profiles) and cost almost nothing. Carried forward as **a sub-component of A1, not a submission**.

## Required improvements (if reworked)

1. **Abandon "is this merchant trustworthy" as the measured claim.** Replace with something checkable: e.g. *"does a published attestation actually correspond to a verifiable external fact"* — GST registration status, corporate registry existence, domain age, policy-page presence — all externally obtainable and falsifiable.
2. If retained as a component, **measure the verifier, not the issuer**: adversarial verification tests (forged signature, expired claim, revoked key, wrong issuer, replayed claim, always-True verifier) are a real, defensible, non-tautological metric with 50+ cases and no invented ground truth.
3. **Never demo a trust verdict about a named real business.**
4. Run and publish the ablation on the LLM signal, and accept the answer.
