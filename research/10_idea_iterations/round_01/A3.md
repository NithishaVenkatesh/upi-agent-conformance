# A3 · The Constraint Layer — eight authority types on a three-field rail

**Judged:** 2026-08-26 · **Panel:** IdeaAgent · **Round:** 01 · **Track:** 01

---

## One-line pitch
A deterministic constraint engine expressing allowed-payees, category, velocity, per-transaction-vs-cumulative, purpose binding and decrement-on-refund on top of UPI Reserve Pay, which natively expresses an amount, a cadence label and a date.

## Problem
The entire spend authority expressible on Razorpay's official agent surface is three fields: `max_amount`, `frequency`, `expire_at`. Verified absent across all 43 MCP tools: allowed payees, MCC scoping, velocity, per-transaction-vs-cumulative distinction, decrement-on-refund, purpose binding, conditional rules, and any way to ask how much of a mandate is left. AP2 specifies eight constraint types verbatim; ACP is worse.

## User
Anyone letting an agent spend on their behalf; the PSP that must underwrite it.

## Solution
A constraint compiler plus enforcement gate: eight constraint types, each with a distinct refusal code, evaluated deterministically before any money call, with a hash-chained ledger and a `remaining_authority` endpoint the rail does not provide.

## AI role
Translate a natural-language spend policy into a candidate constraint set; a deterministic validator accepts or rejects the compilation and the human confirms. AI never evaluates a constraint at runtime.

## Razorpay role
`create_order` with the `single_block_multiple_debit` token block; `fetch_tokens`; `revoke_token`. Delegation/TSP stubbed and declared.

## Competitors
⚠️ **`aryanpajnee/RazorpayBuildathon` — direct, verified, competent, and building this exact thing.** Ed25519 signed Intent + Cart Mandates, canonical JSON, cart hashing, deterministic quote engine in integer paise, GST in basis points, idempotent Razorpay order creation, HMAC webhook verification, 153 real tests, a structurally-enforced no-LLM money path (zero model imports anywhere in `core/` or `merchant/`), specs already written for a 7-check / 14-refusal-code Gate and a hash-chained ledger, and a live `FAILURES.md`. One day of work as of 2026-08-25, with ten days remaining. Assume it lands.

## Judge reaction

**First 10 seconds.** "Three fields is all the authority you can express? That's alarming." Strong opening — the *problem* pitch is arguably the best of the six.

**First 60 seconds.** Everything is clear. Problem, user, solution, why-now. This is the most professionally-framed idea in the pool and it reads like a real engineering proposal. The trouble starts only when the judge remembers what they read this morning.

**After demo.** Remembered: *"the refund gave the budget back and then the blocked purchase went through."* That is a genuinely nice beat. Also remembered, unfortunately: *"this is the second one of these today."*

**Deliberation.**

> **Judge B (senior eng):** Technically this is the most competent thing we've seen. Constraint algebra, refund-aware accounting, hash-chained ledger, race-safe idempotency. He knows what he's doing.
> **Judge C (hackathon):** He also knows what *someone else* is doing. This is the same submission as the other one, minus a hundred and fifty tests.
> **Judge A (product):** Is that fair? Two people independently reaching the same design is evidence the design is right.
> **Judge C:** It's evidence the design is right and evidence that neither of them is differentiated. We have to pick one. Why this one?
> **Judge B:** He says measurement. Let's look. Two hundred synthetic spend attempts scored against a hand-written oracle. What is the oracle?
> **Judge D:** The specification.
> **Judge B:** So the metric is "does my implementation match my specification." That is a unit test. He has written a very large unit test and called it a batch metric. The other candidate wrote a hundred and fifty-three of them and didn't pretend they were a measurement, which is more honest, not less.
> **Judge D (AI judgment):** And on our third pillar, both of them make the same argument — model off the money path — but the other one enforces it with an AST test that fails the build. This one asserts it.
> **Judge A:** The NL-policy compiler is his real AI. Compilation accuracy on a held-out set is a legitimate number.
> **Judge B:** It's a legitimate number for a feature nobody asked for. The compiler runs once, a human confirms it, and if it's wrong the human catches it. He's measuring the least consequential component in his own system because it's the only one he *can* measure.
> **Judge E (chair):** So: best architecture, contested thesis, and the one thing he measures is the one thing that doesn't matter. Where does that place him?
> **Judge C:** Behind the other one.

**Does not survive deliberation.** The killing move is not made by the panel — it is made by the competitor, before the panel sits down.

## Strongest advantage
The clearest, best-evidenced statement of a real production gap in the entire pool, plus the deepest legitimate technical architecture. If judged on engineering alone, this ranks first.

## Strongest weakness
It is the second implementation of a thesis a verified competitor started first and is executing well, and the differentiator proposed (measurement) is not available in this problem shape — because a constraint engine's correctness is a specification-conformance question, and specification conformance is measured with unit tests, not batch metrics.

## Fatal risk

> **The strongest reason this idea loses: there is nothing here to measure honestly, so the one differentiator the corpus identified is unavailable — and without it this is the losing half of a head-to-head against a stronger repo.**

Unpack the metric. "≥200 synthetic spend attempts, authorisation decision accuracy vs a hand-written oracle." The oracle encodes the same eight constraint types the gate encodes, written by the same person, from the same understanding. Accuracy is therefore 100% unless the builder has a bug — at which point the number reports bug density, not system quality. There is no held-out anything, no external anchor, no label that is not a restatement of the rule. It is the field's signature flaw in its purest form, and the corpus's own strategy note ("differentiate on measurement, not concept") accidentally prescribes a differentiator this idea cannot supply.

The escape hatch — measuring NL→constraint compilation accuracy on a held-out set — is a real number about an unimportant component that a human confirms anyway.

**Compounding risk 1 — the competitor.** They have 153 real tests, verified by source read; structural AI exclusion enforced by directory layout; a live `FAILURES.md` (so that edge is gone); specs already written for the Gate and ledger; and a genuinely sharp insight already published (*"a valid signature proves origin, not permission"*). Arriving second with less is not a position.

**Compounding risk 2 — architecture gate.** Whether Razorpay test mode accepts `single_block_multiple_debit` is unverified. This is the idea most dependent on the answer being yes, and it must be resolved on day 1 or the whole build is on sand.

## Competitor saturation
**HIGH.** Not because the space is crowded in general — the bounded-mandate space has few serious occupants — but because it contains one direct, verified, competent occupant aimed at the same judges, in the same track, with the same rubric decoded.

---

## Verdict: **NO-GO**

**Reason: contested head-on by a better-executed competitor, with no available differentiator.** The corpus prescribed measurement as the escape and this problem shape does not admit an honest one. Discard as a submission.

**Salvage:** the refusal-code taxonomy, the `remaining_authority` primitive (genuinely absent from the rail and genuinely useful), and the refund-decrement rule are all excellent *components* and cost little inside another host. Carry them; do not lead with them.

## Required improvements (if fundamentally reworked)

1. **Change what is measured from "does my gate match my spec" to "is my spec's number correct."** That is not a rewording — it is a different project, it has an external ground truth (primary regulatory documents), and it is A4.
2. If the constraint engine is retained at all, **do not claim novelty**, cite the competitor by name in the README, and state precisely what is different. A builder who names their competitor and explains their delta reads far better than one who pretends to be alone.
3. **Resolve the `single_block_multiple_debit` test-mode question by execution on day 1.** Fallback is UPI AutoPay; decide before building.
4. Any AST-enforced no-LLM test must now be treated as **table stakes**, not an edge.
