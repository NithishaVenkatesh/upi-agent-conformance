# B11 · Intent Drift — did the agent buy what you actually asked for?

**Judged:** 2026-08-26 · **Panel:** IdeaAgent · **Round:** 01 · **Track:** 01

---

## One-line pitch
Measure and gate the semantic gap between a human's stated intent and the cart an agent assembled, before the money moves.

## Problem
AP2 names it verbatim as an open problem: *"the merchant knowing the request reflects true intent."* The direct competitor identified the deeper version and left it open: *"`verify()` proves a mandate was signed by the holder of the key it carries and has not changed since. It does not prove that key belongs to anyone entitled to spend… **A valid signature proves origin, not permission.**"* Web Bot Auth reaches the same conclusion from the other side — *"an unresolved Signature-Agent is a claim rather than an identity."* Signature verification is solved and structurally insufficient. Intent conformance is the unsolved half.

## User
Mandate holders; merchants; dispute adjudicators.

## Solution
A conformance scorer over (stated intent, assembled cart) producing a graded verdict — conforming / borderline / divergent — with the divergent dimension named (item, quantity, price, brand, timing), gating the money action on the verdict.

## AI role
**The most load-bearing AI in the entire pool.** Semantic intent conformance is not expressible as a rule: "running shoes under ₹4,000" versus a specific SKU is exactly the residual left after every deterministic check passes. Deliberately not AI: price ceiling, quantity limit, payee allowlist, category — all checked deterministically *first*, so the model only ever sees what arithmetic could not decide. The model's scope is minimised by design and the reduction is measurable.

## Razorpay role
Thin. The gate sits immediately before `create_order`; server-side amount resolution is the backstop.

## Competitors
No direct Buildathon competitor found. Globally, LLM-as-judge for agent trajectory and cart conformance is an active and crowded research/tooling genre.

## Strongest advantage
The only idea in the pool where the LLM is unambiguously irreplaceable, combined with the only measurement design that voluntarily separates out the portion of its own headline that deterministic checks already decided. That separation is a genuinely elite move — it is the builder attacking his own number before anyone else does, which is the single behaviour the corpus identifies as absent from the entire field.

## Strongest weakness
Razorpay fit of 46. Everything that makes this idea good is portable to any payment processor on earth, and a hiring panel at a payments company reads "portable" as "not about us."

## Fatal risk

> **The strongest reason this idea loses: it is a superb answer to a question Razorpay did not ask, evaluated on a corpus the builder wrote himself.**

**Primary kill — relevance.** The role's rule is explicit and this is its textbook case. Strip the `create_order` call and nothing about the project changes. There is no UPI, no NPCI ceiling, no rail asymmetry, no PSP-only signal, no India. Against a field where other candidates are demonstrating things that only make sense in Indian payments, a globally-portable agent-eval project reads as a candidate who wanted to build what they wanted to build. That is a problem-taste finding, not a relevance technicality.

**Secondary kill — the corpus.** 150 hand-authored (intent, cart) pairs means the builder wrote the intent, wrote the divergence, and wrote the label. The ground truth is the authoring process. The stated defence — reporting separately on the deterministic residual — is a genuinely good mitigation for *arithmetic* contamination and no mitigation at all for *authorship* contamination. The fix exists and is cheap: use the four live Indian merchant catalogues as the item universe and let a real buyer agent assemble the carts, so the drift is **discovered rather than designed**. That converts the target from self-generated to observed and would move this from 79 to the high 80s globally.

**Tertiary risk — LLM grading LLM.** The subject and the judge are the same model family, so their errors correlate: a buyer agent that misreads the intent will produce a cart a judge with the same misreading approves. Nothing in the design breaks that correlation, and this system gates money.

## Competitor saturation
**LOW** within the Buildathon field. **HIGH** globally — LLM-as-judge conformance gating is a heavily-populated 2026 genre, and a judge who works on agents will place it instantly.

---

## Verdict: **NO-GO as a standalone submission · GO as a component**

**Reason for the standalone NO-GO:** Razorpay fit of 46 fails the role's explicit two-axis rule, and no amount of build quality repairs it — the deficiency is in what the project is *about*, not in how well it is made.

**Reason for the component GO:** this is the best available answer to "where is the AI load-bearing," which is precisely the question that wounds A1 and A3. Bolted on to a Razorpay-anchored host — as the final semantic gate after the deterministic clause-cited bounds have passed — it supplies exactly the pillar-3 content those ideas lack, at low marginal cost. It should be built **only if the host lands first**, and never at the expense of the host.

## Required improvements

1. **Acquire an external item universe.** Use the four verified live Indian merchant catalogues. The moment the cart is assembled from a real catalogue by a real model, the drift becomes observed rather than authored, and the headline number becomes defensible.
2. **Let a real buyer agent generate the divergences.** Do not write them. The best cases will be ones the builder did not anticipate — and those cases are also the raw material for the failure narrative that is read first.
3. **Break the grader/subject correlation**: different model family for the judge than for the buyer, and report the agreement rate between them as its own number.
4. **Price the false refusal.** A gate that blocks correct purchases has a cost; publish the cost-weighted FP/FN table and state the operating threshold that follows from it.
5. **If used as a component, anchor it to India explicitly** — intents in Hinglish, Indian brand and category ambiguity, ₹ price bands under the ₹5,000 per-transaction ceiling. That is a cheap way to convert portable work into local work.
6. **Keep the residual-vs-full-set split.** It is the best thing in the design and it must stay on the README's first screen.
