# B1 · The Empty Quadrant — a feasibility oracle for Indian agent payments

**Judged:** 2026-08-26 · **Panel:** IdeaAgent · **Round:** 01 · **Track:** 01

---

## One-line pitch
Answer, for any proposed agent-payment scenario, whether it is legally executable in India today — and if not, exactly which clause stops it and what the nearest legal alternative is.

## Problem
*"At most ₹5,000 per transaction and ₹15,000 per month, domestic P2M only, as one of five delegates, inside a CUG pilot — and delegation removes the UPI PIN, not the human."* The higher-value alternative, UPI AutoPay, buys its limits by fixing the payee in advance so the agent has no discretion, and the 2026 Master Direction forbids customer-set sub-limits on it. **The quadrant everyone wants — high cap and agent discretion — is empty, and it is precisely the quadrant ACP's `delegate_payment` assumes exists.** Almost nobody building in this space knows any of it.

## User
Every builder, PM and compliance reviewer designing an agentic payment product for India — and, implicitly, the panel.

## Solution
A scenario evaluator: plain-language use case → structured scenario → rule engine over the encoded circular set → verdict (`EXECUTABLE` / `EXECUTABLE_WITH_CONSTRAINTS` / `NOT_AUTHORISED`) + exact citation + nearest legal variant + a machine-readable constraint block.

## AI role
Natural-language scenario → structured parameters (amount, cadence, payee scope, delegate count, domestic/cross-border, P2M/P2P, human-trigger presence). Explicitly: *"the model is a parser."* Deliberately not AI: the rule set, the evaluation, the verdict, the citations.

## Razorpay role
The rule set is Razorpay's operating envelope; outputs are consumable as Razorpay MCP `create_order` token blocks.

## Competitors
None found. ⚠️ Every GitHub hit for "NPCI UAP" is another Buildathon entrant — circular evidence.

### Razorpay fit — scored separately

**68 / 100.** Real but middling. The rule set genuinely is Razorpay's operating envelope and the output genuinely exports as an MCP token block, which is a thoughtful touch. But the artifact is a knowledge product about regulation, not a payments system: it does not move money, does not touch a rail, does not use test mode, and does not exercise any Razorpay capability. A Razorpay panel sees a well-researched advisory tool about the environment they operate in, rather than something built on what they operate.

## Judge reaction

**First 10 seconds.** "Tell me if my agent-payment idea is legal in India, with the clause." Understood immediately. Also immediately filed as "compliance tool," which is a category that has to climb back up.

**First 60 seconds.** Clear on all four counts. The why-now is strong. The moment of real interest is a fact, not a feature: *an AI agent cannot legally be a full-delegation secondary because OC-201A requires a KYC-identified family member or employee.* Judges lean in for that sentence and then realise the sentence came from reading, not from building.

**After demo.** Remembered: **the empty quadrant**. Not the system. This is the diagnostic problem — the memory is of the research, and the research would have been just as memorable in a blog post.

**Deliberation.**

> **Judge A (product):** Genuinely useful. I'd send it to our compliance team.
> **Judge C (hackathon):** Would you send it to them as a product or as a document?
> **Judge A:** …as a document.
> **Judge C:** That's the problem. The best thing in this submission is a finding, and a finding isn't a build.
> **Judge B (senior eng):** And we saw the same rule table an hour ago, in the one that fails the build on a real PR. Same circulars, same OCR, same citations. That one enforces at the money boundary. This one prints advice.
> **Judge D (AI judgment):** The model here is a parser. He says so himself, which I respect, but it means his LLM is filling in eight form fields and asking the user to confirm. That's not a judgment call, that's a form.
> **Judge B:** And then look at the metric. A hundred scenarios he wrote, labelled by reading circulars he encoded into the engine. The engine and the label share a source and an author. If he misread OC-201B, he misread it in the rules and in the labels, and scores himself right.
> **Judge D: **The other one has the same annotator issue but at least its labels are sentences from a document it didn't write. Here the *scenarios* are invented too. Two layers of authorship instead of one.
> **Judge A:** There's a liability angle nobody's mentioned. It renders legal verdicts. Would any of us actually rely on it?
> **Judge B:** No. And if the answer is no, what is it for?
> **Judge E (chair):** Then it's a very good piece of research with a thin application attached, and we already have its stronger twin.

**Does not survive deliberation.** It is eliminated by comparison rather than by defect — which is the most common way a competent idea dies.

## Strongest advantage
It sits on top of the single best piece of research in the corpus, its UX instinct (show the parse back before evaluating) is correct and doubles as its safety mechanism, and its adversarial boundary cases (₹4,999 vs ₹5,001, delegate 5 vs 6, P2P vs P2M, ₹2,000 vs ₹5,000 first-24h) show real care about where a rule engine actually breaks.

## Strongest weakness
It is the weaker front-end of an engine whose stronger front-end is also on the table, and it makes the one choice that costs it most: it demotes the model to a parser in a domain where a genuinely hard model task was available and known.

## Fatal risk

> **The strongest reason this idea loses: it is dominated by A4 — same engine, same sources, same effort, strictly weaker on AI necessity, impact and demo — and the panel will have seen A4.**

Domination is the cleanest form of loss and it is unambiguous here. Both encode the same circulars from the same scanned PDFs behind the same 403. A4 gives the model an irreplaceable job (joint value/unit/scope extraction, with a naive baseline that reproduces a real shipped bug) and enforces the result at a money boundary by failing a build and refusing a payment. B1 gives the model form-filling and enforces nothing. There is no axis on which B1 wins except that it is slightly easier to finish, and "easier to finish" is not a differentiator to present to a panel.

**Second, independent kill — the doubled ground truth.** A4's labels are sentences from documents it did not write. B1's *scenarios* are invented by the builder **and** its labels come from the same circular reading that produced the rule engine. Author, rules and labels share one head. The 30-second attack — "your evaluator and your answer key are the same understanding of the same document" — lands harder here than on any other idea except A3.

**Third risk — the research/build inversion.** The most memorable thing in the submission is the empty-quadrant finding, which required no code. In a competition whose stated premise is *"your code speaks louder than your resume,"* being remembered for your reading is a soft failure.

**Fourth risk — liability shape.** A system that issues legal verdicts about payment schemes invites the question "would you rely on this?", and the honest answer is no. A tool whose author would not rely on it is hard to defend for five minutes.

## Competitor saturation
**LOW** in this field. **MODERATE** globally — regulatory Q&A with citation grounding is a common and well-understood application shape, and several judges will have evaluated one before.

---

## Verdict: **NO-GO**

**Reason: dominated by A4 on the same engine, and its AI role is deliberately demoted to parsing in a domain that offered a genuinely hard model task.** The corpus already recorded the correct decision — *"A4 ≈ B1: one rule engine, two front-ends. Do not build both."* This is the front-end to drop.

**What survives:** the empty-quadrant analysis is a superb *framing* asset and belongs in the README and the pitch video of whatever does get built. The `EXECUTABLE / EXECUTABLE_WITH_CONSTRAINTS / NOT_AUTHORISED` verdict taxonomy and the nearest-legal-variant suggestion are both good and cost almost nothing to include as an output mode of A4's engine. The parse-then-confirm interaction pattern should be reused wherever a model's structured output feeds a money decision.

## Required improvements (if it were kept instead of A4)

1. **Give the model the hard job.** In this exact domain the hard job is clause extraction with scope classification — the discrimination Razorpay's own engineers got wrong. Taking it converts B1 into A4, which is the honest conclusion.
2. **Enforce something.** An advisory verdict must become a refusal at a money boundary, or the submission is a document.
3. **Break the authorship loop.** Source scenarios externally — real questions from developer forums, real integration decisions in public PRs, the six Razorpay ACP PRs — rather than inventing them.
4. **Never present it as legal advice.** State the reliance limits before being asked.
