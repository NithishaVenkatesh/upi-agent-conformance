# A1 · `in.razorpay.upi` — the UPI payment handler UCP is waiting for

**Judged:** 2026-08-26 · **Panel:** IdeaAgent · **Round:** 01 · **Track:** 01

---

## One-line pitch
Publish the missing UCP payment handler that lets an AI agent pay an Indian merchant by UPI, plus a conformance harness that decides whether a handler declaration is honest.

## Problem
Four major Indian D2C brands serve live UCP `2026-04-08` profiles declaring exactly two payment handlers — `com.google.pay` and `dev.shopify.card` — and not one accepts UPI. Even the Google Pay handler is `"type": "CARD"`. An agent shopping at an Indian brand, for an Indian customer, in a country where UPI is 80%+ of digital payments, can pay by Visa, Mastercard, Amex, Discover or Diners Club and nothing else.

## User
Indian D2C merchants receiving agent traffic; the PSP that wants to be the rail underneath it.

## Solution
(a) An `in.razorpay.upi` handler declaration conforming to `ucp.dev/specification/payment-handler-guide`; (b) a reference merchant serving it at `/.well-known/ucp`; (c) a buyer agent that discovers it and completes a UPI payment in Razorpay test mode; (d) a conformance harness that fetches any merchant's UCP profile and checks the handler declaration against the published guide and the NPCI/RBI ceilings.

## AI role
Buyer-side goal decomposition, handler selection under ambiguity, natural-language explanation of a conformance failure. Deliberately not AI: schema validation, integer-paise arithmetic, signature verification, limit enforcement, the pass/fail verdict.

## Razorpay role
Maximal. Test-mode order creation and UPI payment; the handler is named for and specified by Razorpay as the regional PSP; RTB/KYC as the merchant-side trust input; and it walks straight through the door ACP has kept Razorpay outside of for 3.5 months — UCP invites a regional PSP to publish a handler with no committee approval.

## Competitors
`Adarsh-Me/Agent-Audit` (the only serious sell-side repo; its committed evidence is a deterministic mock with a rerun delta of exactly zero). `aryanpajnee/RazorpayBuildathon` (mandate layer, not handler layer — adjacent, not overlapping). The catalogue-legibility genre is crowded and solved; the payment-handler lane is empty.

## Judge reaction

**First 10 seconds.** "Wait — none of them take UPI? In India?" Immediate lean-in. This is the strongest ten-second pitch of the six.

**First 60 seconds.** Problem, user and why-now are all clear and evidenced. Solution is clear. The one thing that is *not* clear at 60 seconds is where the intelligence is — a careful listener notices that everything described so far is JSON, schema validation and a payment call.

**After demo.** What is remembered: *"four live Indian merchants, card only."* That single fact is the whole memory. What is **not** remembered is the conformance harness, the batch metric, or anything the model did. The demo's own strength buries the rest of the submission.

**Deliberation.** Five judges, after seeing everything else:

> **Judge C (hackathon):** This one I remember. The curl demo is real and it took him ten seconds to prove it in front of us.
> **Judge B (senior eng):** Prove *what*, though. That four Shopify storefronts emit a Shopify-generated profile. Shopify generated those, not the merchants. He found a Shopify default, not an Indian payments failure.
> **Judge A (product):** That is a fair hit but it does not kill it — the Shopify default *is* the reason Indian merchants can't take agent UPI. The mechanism doesn't matter to the customer.
> **Judge B:** Then look at the number. Sixty profiles, four real, fifty-six he mutated himself. He is measuring whether his validator detects mutations he wrote. The label is the mutation script. That is the same tautology we caught in three other submissions today, dressed better.
> **Judge D (AI judgment):** And where is the model? Handler selection with two candidates. Explaining a failure the validator already localised. If I delete the LLM, what breaks?
> **Judge A:** Nothing breaks. Which by our own rubric is not automatically a fault — pillar three rewards *not* using a model. But he has to argue it, and he can't, because he *did* use one and it isn't earning.
> **Judge E (chair):** So: best problem, best demo, weakest measurement, ornamental AI. That is a strong second round, not a shortlist lock. What did he actually *verify*?

**Deliberation survives — but wounded.** The disagreement is real and does not average away: Judge C rates it top-3 on demo; Judge B rates it mid-field on evidence. That split is the honest output.

## Strongest advantage
A verified, judge-reproducible, ten-second fact that nobody else in the field has, sitting at the exact intersection of Razorpay's institutional identity (UPI, India, PSP) and a standards door that is provably open to them. Nothing else in the pool has a "before" state a judge can check from their own laptop while the video is playing.

## Strongest weakness
The AI is decorative and the builder cannot honestly claim otherwise. Every load-bearing decision in this system is deterministic — correctly deterministic — which means pillar 3's first half ("the right tool in the right place") has nothing to point at.

## Fatal risk

> **The strongest reason this idea loses: the headline number is a tautology, and the LLM is ornamental — two independent kills, either of which is sufficient.**

**Kill 1 — the measurement target.** "Conformance-verdict precision/recall over 4 real + 56 mutated profiles" measures whether a schema validator detects violations the same author injected. The ground truth *is* the mutation script. A depth-1 decision stump over "does field X exist" will score ~1.00, which is the exact adversarial-baseline result the corpus says kills every measured repo in the field. The builder would be publishing the field's signature flaw with better paperwork.

**Kill 2 — pillar 3.** Delete the model: the handler still publishes, the validator still validates, the payment still completes. The only casualty is prose. Razorpay's rubric explicitly grades "the right tool in the right place, **and where you chose not to use one**" — and a system that used one where it wasn't needed fails the first half while looking like it passes the second.

**Secondary risk.** All four "merchants" run Shopify, which auto-generates UCP profiles. A sharp judge will ask whether the finding is about Indian merchants or about Shopify's default handler set. The honest answer is Shopify — and the honest follow-up is that this is *why* it matters, but the builder must reach that answer before the judge does, not after.

## Competitor saturation
**LOW** on the handler layer specifically (verified empty). **EXTREMELY SATURATED** on the adjacent catalogue-legibility layer (Shopify auto-generates profiles; ≥20 sell-side Buildathon repos). The risk is not that someone else builds A1 — it is that a judge mistakes A1 for the saturated neighbour in the first fifteen seconds.

---

## Verdict: **GO**

**Weakest dimension to fix: AI necessity (3/8).** It is not fixable by adding more model calls. It is fixable only by importing a job the model is irreplaceable at — and there is exactly one such job available in this problem space: extracting the regulatory ceilings a handler declaration must be checked against, from scanned NPCI circulars, with correct scope classification. That is A4. **A1 is the best product surface in the pool and A4 is the best AI core in the pool, and neither is complete alone.**

## Required improvements

1. **Replace the measurement target.** Self-mutated profiles cannot be the headline. Options, in descending order of strength: (a) measure conformance against *externally sourced* declarations — the six real Razorpay ACP PRs, the four live profiles, the MCP token schema, published handler guides; (b) measure whether a declaration is *honest* by executing against it, so the ground truth is a payment outcome and not a schema opinion.
2. **Run the adversarial baseline hunt before the judge does.** Publish the depth-1/2/3 stump table. If the stump matches the system, say so on the README's first screen and change the target.
3. **Fix or remove the LLM.** Either give it a job it is irreplaceable at, or delete it and write the `WHERE_WE_DID_NOT_USE_AI.md` argument with a cost column — a fully deterministic A1 with a rigorous non-use argument scores *higher* on pillar 3 than the current design.
4. **Pre-empt the Shopify objection in the README**, in the first screen, in the builder's own words.
5. **Do not claim adoption.** "Publishing a spec-conformant handler and a conformance harness is the contribution; merchant adoption is not in our gift" — say it explicitly, before being asked.
6. **Day-1 kill-gate:** acquire test keys (business type *Individual*, not Sole Proprietorship, reveals "Get test keys") and execute one UPI test payment end-to-end before writing anything else.
