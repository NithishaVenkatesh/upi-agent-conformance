## The T05 trap, addressed once up front

Track 05's bar, verbatim: *"Show a real problem, **a working product**, meaningful use of AI, and evidence that it creates value."* A naked evaluation harness reads as a tool, not a solution.

**The structural answer used throughout this pool:** every idea ships **a product that performs a money-touching or money-deciding job**, and the measurement machinery is the thing that *licenses the product to act autonomously*. The harness is not a sidecar — it is the gate. The product cannot take an unsupervised action that the measurement has not earned. That framing converts "I built an eval suite" into "I built a system whose autonomy is metered by its own proven accuracy," which is a direct read of the Track 01/03 bar (*"explainable, bounded and gated"* / *"compliant escalation, stopping rules"*). Each idea below states its own version of this in the `Product-not-tool` line.

## 01 · TAUTOLOGY — the leakage gate that refuses to let a model ship

**Pitch.** A pre-deployment gate that tries to break your risk model's headline number before you publish it, and blocks the model from earning autonomy if a trivial rule gets within a whisker of it.

**Problem.** A merchant risk team ships a gradient-boosted RTO/fraud scorer with a beautiful AUC, unaware that a single feature reconstructs the label. The model is then trusted with money decisions it did not earn.

**Target user.** Merchant risk/ML engineer; the internal reviewer who has to sign off on an autonomous decline.

**Why it matters (corpus).** This is the **field-wide fatal flaw**, verified in every measured repo (`FIELD_BAR.md` §2 Opening 1). The single loudest instance: `komallbarhate` — `days_to_estimated <= 2` alone beats an entire LightGBM+TreeSHAP stack by **$8,455** on the author's own cost matrix. And `SaxenaLakshya` advertised *"cost-weighted evaluation"* while containing **no cost matrix, no confusion matrix, and no C_FP/C_FN anywhere** (`FIELD_BAR.md` §4). Only `vaibhav375` defends against circularity at all, and only for its causal tier. Independently, RBI has conceded fraud is an AI problem (MuleHunter.ai, 23 banks live 17 Dec 2025 — `08_market/market_problems.md` #5), so *bad* fraud models will get deployed at scale.

**Solution.** A CLI + service that takes `(features, label, split)` and runs an escalating adversarial baseline hunt: majority class → depth-1 stump → depth-2 → depth-3 → single-feature threshold sweep → regex over any text column → leave-one-feature-out. It emits a **Tautology Report**: the best trivial rule, its score, the gap to your model, and a verdict (`EARNED` / `MARGINAL` / `TAUTOLOGICAL`). Wired into the deployment path, a `TAUTOLOGICAL` verdict revokes the model's autonomy tier — it may still score, but only into a human queue.

**AI role.** LLM reads the *feature dictionary and label definition in natural language* and flags semantic circularity a stump cannot find — e.g. "`days_to_estimated` is derived from the same delivery event that generates the survey that produces the label." This is genuinely LLM-shaped: it is reasoning over documentation, not data.
**Where deliberately NOT AI.** The entire stump/sweep/ablation engine is `sklearn` + numpy. No LLM anywhere in the scoring path, enforced by an AST test that fails the build on any `openai`/`anthropic` import inside `gate/` (pattern from `vaibhav375 tests/test_kernel_no_llm_imports.py`, with its vacuity guard). **Cost of that choice:** we cannot catch leakage that is only visible in the joint distribution and has no low-depth tree expression; we say so.

**Razorpay role.** Ships as the pre-flight check for any model scoring Razorpay payment/order data; test-mode order + payment objects supply the feature schema. Directly serves the T02 bar's *"honest metrics including false-positive cost."*

**Key workflow.** register model → gate runs 7 attack families → report → autonomy tier assigned → tier is enforced at decision time → any re-train re-runs the gate.

**The batch metric.** **Detection rate on planted leakage over 60+ synthetic model/dataset pairs.** We construct 30 datasets with a deliberately planted leak (of six distinct leak archetypes: bijection, derived-feature, temporal, threshold-echo, target-encoding, ID-ordering) and 30 clean ones, then report **precision/recall of the gate's TAUTOLOGICAL verdict, with a confidence interval, and per-archetype breakdown**. This is a *planted-ground-truth instrument validation* — the label is ours by construction and therefore honest, because we are measuring the detector, not the phenomenon.

**Defence against B1–B5.** B1: the label *is* "did we plant a leak," which cannot leak. B2: baseline is "always say clean" and "always say leaky," both reported. B3: ablate each of the 7 attack families and re-report — if removing the regex family changes nothing, say the regex family is inert. B4: attrition ledger of datasets that failed to generate/fit, reconciled to the denominator. B5: n/a (no regulatory constraint claimed).

**Product-not-tool.** The product is an autonomy gate on a live scoring service, not a report generator. Demoable as: model requests auto-decline authority, gate denies it, model is downgraded to advisory.

**Technical depth.** Leak archetype taxonomy, adversarial search over feature subsets, Wilson CI on detection rate, deterministic seeding, AST enforcement.

**Demo.** Feed it `komallbarhate`-shaped data. Watch it print: *"A depth-1 stump on `days_to_estimated` scores 0.9435. Your model scores 0.8615. Autonomy DENIED."* Then feed it clean data and watch it grant.

**Differentiation.** Nobody in the 261-repo field does this. It is the *"single highest-value artifact available to us"* per `FIELD_BAR.md` §2.

**Competitors.** `deepchecks`, `great_expectations` (schema, not adversarial-baseline). No Buildathon rival.

**Track.** Primary **T02** · Secondary T05.

## 02 · STRAWMAN — a recovery agent that must beat three baselines it did not design

**Pitch.** A failed-payment recovery agent whose recovered-rupees headline is only reported alongside a do-nothing arm, a *tuned* naive retry arm, and a randomised holdout that was never contacted.

**Problem.** Every recovery uplift number in the field is measured against a baseline the same author built to lose. The merchant then over-attributes recovery to the agent and under-invests in the cheap fix.

**Target user.** Merchant growth/payments ops; the finance person who has to defend the ROI of the agent.

**Why it matters (corpus).** `shubhambhattog`'s **6.38× uplift** is two constants the same author chose: baseline retries at t/+1h/+2h (`baseline.ts:27`) while funds arrive at t+12–96h (`generate.ts:80`) — **0% recovery by construction**, undisclosed in `SIMULATION.md` §4 (`FIELD_BAR.md` §2 Opening 2). `komallbarhate`'s rule baseline scores P=0.003 because it has **the wrong sign** on the dominant feature. And this matters *specifically to this panel*: **Razorpay ships Smart Retry**, so a straw-man retry baseline is the fastest possible way to lose credibility (`FIELD_BAR.md` §2 Opening 2, `THE_REAL_RUBRIC.md` §6 — Agent Studio ships Subscription Recovery). The underlying problem is the best-evidenced in the corpus: **all 10 publicly-visible banks in NPCI's July 2026 remitter data breach OC-149's 5% business-decline target; 5 exceed 10%; Airtel Payments Bank at 26.97% BD; volume-weighted 12.52% BD vs 0.39% TD** (`market_problems.md` #1, Dataful #445 sourcing NPCI).

**Solution.** A recovery engine with a **first-class experiment layer**: every failed payment is randomised at intake into `{never-contact holdout, B0 do-nothing, B1 tuned-naive, TREATMENT}`. B1's parameters are *tuned by grid search on the training window to maximise B1's own recovery* — i.e. we optimise our competitor. Reported metric is **incremental** recovery vs holdout, not gross.

**AI role.** Root-cause diagnosis over decline reason codes + bank/instrument/time context, producing a *ranked intervention plan* (rail switch, retry window, channel, message). This is genuinely LLM-shaped: it is many-to-many reasoning over a messy code space (`track_scorecard.md` T03: "root-cause diagnosis over failure codes is genuinely LLM-shaped").
**Where deliberately NOT AI.** Randomisation, arm assignment, stopping rules, money movement, and the incrementality arithmetic are all deterministic. Per `tfthushaar calibrator.py:1-15`, **deterministic arithmetic results are reported as exact and never get a confidence interval** — running a CI over a provably-correct computation is conceptually wrong. **Cost:** the tuned baseline B1 stays dumber than an LLM could make it; we deliberately do not LLM-tune B1 because that would make the comparison incoherent.

**Razorpay role.** Test-mode payment failure injection; real Razorpay decline reason codes as the feature space; Smart Retry named explicitly as the *baseline we are trying to beat*, not ignored.

**Key workflow.** failure webhook → arm assignment → (treatment: diagnose → plan → bounded execute) → outcome capture → nightly incrementality report with CI.

**The batch metric.** **Incremental recovery rate, treatment minus never-contacted holdout, over 500+ simulated failures, with a bootstrap CI, reported alongside B0 and B1** — and reported as **₹ per attempt**, not gross ₹, so the denominator is visible.

**Defence.** B1: the outcome label is a simulated funds-arrival event generated *independently* of the feature vector, and we publish the generator's coupling assumptions in a table. B2: this *is* the B2 defence — three baselines, one of them tuned against us. B3: ablate the LLM diagnosis (replace with round-robin intervention) and re-report; if uplift survives, the LLM is decorative and we say so. B4: attrition ledger `N_failures → N_eligible → N_contacted → N_outcome_observed`, with stopping-rule and opt-out drops named. B5: stopping rules cite RBI's e-mandate pre-debit notification requirement (*"at least 24 hours prior"*, RBI/DPSS/2026-27/396, 21 Apr 2026 — `market_problems.md` #9) rather than inventing a cadence.

**Product-not-tool.** It recovers money. The experiment layer is how it proves it did.

**Technical depth.** Stratified randomisation, cluster bootstrap for correlated retries (`Adarsh-Me stats/bootstrap.py:15-63` pattern), sequential stopping, opt-out ledger.

**Demo.** Two dashboards side by side: "Gross recovered: ₹4,12,000" vs "Incremental vs holdout: ₹61,400 (95% CI ₹38k–₹84k)." Say out loud that the first number is the one everyone else will show.

**Differentiation.** 24% of the field is in T03; the differentiator is the holdout, which almost none will have.

**Competitors.** Razorpay Smart Retry (production); `vaibhav375/recovery-ledger` is the only field repo doing incremental accounting with a no-contact holdout (`INDEX.md` pattern #8) — **this idea is directly contested by the field's strongest repo**.

**Track.** Primary **T03** · Secondary T05.

## 03 · INERT — the ablation harness that tells a merchant which of their fraud signals do nothing

**Pitch.** A fraud/risk stack that automatically deletes each of its own signals in turn, re-measures, and publishes the ones that turned out not to matter.

**Problem.** Multi-signal risk systems accumulate features nobody has ever tested the marginal value of. The merchant pays for data feeds that contribute zero.

**Target user.** Merchant risk lead deciding whether to renew a device-fingerprinting or address-verification vendor contract.

**Why it matters (corpus).** `FIELD_BAR.md` §2 Opening 6: **not one of the ten repos deletes a component and re-measures.** When the analysis did it for them: `MrBurber/KinGraph` — deleting **all identifier edges**, the device/IP/address graph the project is *named for* — reproduces **100/100/0 byte-identically**. At `min_edge_weight=3.0` the `+3` bonus (`detect_rings.py:12`) lets a single embedding edge pass alone while genuine households at weight 2 are dropped. **The entire premise is inert and the author never checked.** Same file also records `komallbarhate`'s stack losing to one subtraction and `SaxenaLakshya`'s depth-3 tree. The market context: card/internet fraud is **66.8% of India's bank-reported fraud cases (7,756) but only 7.2% of value (₹252 cr)** — RBI *Trend & Progress 2024-25* (`market_problems.md` #5) — i.e. high-frequency, low-value, exactly where signal cost per decision matters.

**Solution.** A defense-only risk scorer built as a registry of independently-toggleable signals. A nightly `ablate` job removes each signal (and each pair, budget permitting), re-runs the held-out evaluation, and writes a **Marginal Value Table**: Δprecision, Δrecall, Δcost, and a ₹/month feed cost column. Signals with a Δ indistinguishable from zero are flagged `INERT` and can be switched off with one click — that is the product.

**AI role.** LLM writes the *narrative* for each inert finding — "your address-similarity signal is subsumed by pincode + ticket-size; here is the overlap" — from the ablation numbers. Advisory only.
**Where deliberately NOT AI.** Signal computation, ablation orchestration, all metrics. **Cost:** we cannot detect a signal that is inert *on this data distribution* but valuable under an attack we have not simulated; the report says so explicitly and refuses to recommend deletion of any signal whose removal we could not test adversarially.

**Razorpay role.** Signals drawn from what a PSP actually sees: instrument, issuer, decline history, order velocity, address, device — the structural advantage of sitting on the rail.

**Key workflow.** score → nightly ablate → marginal value table → merchant switches off inert signals → re-baseline.

**The batch metric.** **Held-out precision/recall with cost-weighted expected loss over a 2,000+ transaction test set, reported once per signal-subset**, plus **the ablation table itself as the headline artifact** — including every row where the metric did not move. Per `FIELD_BAR.md`: *"keep any row where the metric doesn't move. That row is worth more than the ones that do."*

**Defence.** B1: an adversarial baseline hunt (idea 01's engine) runs first; the label is a simulated fraud outcome whose generator is published, and we report the best depth-3 stump next to the model. B2: majority-class, single-best-signal, and a tuned rules engine. B3: this **is** the B3 idea. B4: attrition ledger of transactions dropped for missing signals — and crucially, **the effective n changes per ablation run**, which we report per row. B5: defense-only framing cited against Track 02's only explicit disqualifier.

**Product-not-tool.** A scorer that makes live decline/review decisions; the ablation is what tells the merchant what to stop paying for.

**Technical depth.** Signal registry with dependency graph, combinatorial ablation with budget control, paired bootstrap for Δ significance, cost matrix.

**Demo.** "Here is our fraud graph. Here is the same system with the entire graph deleted. The number moved by 0.3 points. We are shipping it anyway, but we are telling you it barely earns its keep."

**Differentiation.** Publishing your own null result is the rarest thing in the field. `Sivanandini` published an inconvenient cost finding (`INDEX.md` pattern #19) and it was the best thing in that repo.

**Competitors.** No field rival. Adjacent: SHAP-based importance (`komallbarhate`) — but importance is not ablation, and the corpus shows importance passing while the component is inert.

**Track.** Primary **T02** · Secondary T04.

## 04 · ATTRITION — the reconciliation agent whose product is the exception list

**Pitch.** A four-way reconciliation agent that leads with what it could *not* match, and reconciles `N_in → N_matched → N_reported` to the rupee.

**Problem.** Reconciliation tools report a match rate and bury the residue. The residue is where the money is.

**Target user.** Merchant finance controller at monthly close.

**Why it matters (corpus).** Track 04's bar names it verbatim: *"Throughput plus measured accuracy plus **an honest exception list**. One cherry-picked match proves nothing."* And the field's failure mode is precisely denominator opacity: `Adarsh-Me` headlines "640 trials" against **234 parse_ok (36.6%)**, disclosed only in `Docs/SUMMARY.md:115`; `tfthushaar` headlines 50,000 against a real-LLM batch of **120**; `shubhambhattog`'s `postOptOutContacts` is a **hardcoded 0 wired to a CI gate** (`FIELD_BAR.md` §2 Opening 5). The problem itself is the corpus's best *demo*: **ERP has orders; bank has net credits; PG report has transactions-minus-MDR; marketplace payout has sales − commission − TDS(194-O 1%) − GST TCS − returns. No two share a primary key, granularity, or timestamp convention** (`market_problems.md` #6) — and honestly, *no credible statistic exists for its magnitude, and that is the honest finding*, which is itself a differentiator when everyone else is quoting vendor numbers.

**Solution.** Deterministic matching cascade (exact → tolerance → many-to-many netting → temporal window), with every unmatched record entering a typed exception queue. The headline artifact is an **Attrition Ledger**: every record that entered, every record dropped, the reason, and a rupee reconciliation that must sum to zero or the build fails.

**AI role.** LLM operates *only on the residual* — proposing a hypothesis for an exception ("this is a batched settlement net of a 1% TDS deduction across 14 orders") which is then **replayed arithmetically and discarded if it does not reconcile**. Validate-or-discard, not retry (pattern from `MrBurber evidence_agent.py`, `INDEX.md` #17).
**Where deliberately NOT AI.** All matching, all arithmetic, the ledger. Per Track 04's own why-now, this is the verification bottleneck; per `tfthushaar calibrator.py:1-15`, deterministic results are reported exact with no CI. **Cost:** the deterministic cascade will refuse fuzzy matches a human would accept; our match rate is lower than a fuzzier system's and we say so with an example.

**Razorpay role.** Settlement report, payment objects, Route splits, and test-mode webhooks are four of the four sources. This is Razorpay's structural data advantage used directly.

**Key workflow.** ingest 4 sources → normalise → deterministic cascade → exception typing → LLM hypothesis on residual → arithmetic replay → accept/reject → ledger + close pack.

**The batch metric.** **Match rate and per-type exception counts over a 1,000+ record synthetic batch (well past the bar's 50), with the attrition ledger reconciling to ₹0 residual**, plus **LLM hypothesis precision on the residual measured against planted ground truth** — we generate the batch with known true correspondences, so the label is ours, and we publish the generator.

**Defence.** B1: because the generator plants the correspondence, we must prove the matcher is not just reading the generator — so we run an adversarial pass where a depth-3 stump on record features tries to predict "will match", and publish the result. B2: baselines are exact-key-only, and a naive amount+date join. B3: ablate each cascade stage; if the temporal-window stage adds nothing, publish that. B4: this **is** the B4 idea. B5: TDS 194-O at 1% and GST TCS rates are cited to statute, not assumed, with the citation in the code comment adjacent to the constant — a direct guard against the SEP-#216 failure mode.

**Product-not-tool.** It produces a signed monthly close pack a controller can act on.

**Technical depth.** Many-to-many netting, tolerance algebra in integer paise, hash-chained ledger tested in *both* directions (`vaibhav375 test_ledger_tamper.py:1-8` — "a function that always returns False passes a naive test").

**Demo.** Four files on screen that visibly cannot be joined. Then the residual. Then the ₹0.

**Differentiation.** 10% of the field is in T04 and at least nine named rivals are doing reconciliation (`REPO_SHORTLIST.md` rival section). The differentiator is the ledger reconciling to zero and the both-directions tamper test.

**Competitors.** Heavy — `tfthushaar`, `Samyak17Jain/reconciliation-sentinel`, `ch24btech11028-create/recoagent`, `cloudavenue0012-creator/settlement-reconciliation-engine`, `JazR20/reckon`, `simpleciki/unreconciled`. **This is the most crowded idea in the pool.**

**Track.** Primary **T04** · Secondary T05.

## 05 · CLAUSE — a mandate gate where every spend bound must cite the clause that authorises it

**Pitch.** An agentic-payment authorisation gate in which no numeric limit may exist in code unless it carries a machine-checkable citation to the regulatory clause it came from — and a conformance test that fails the build when the code and the cited clause disagree.

**Problem.** Constraint claims drift from the regulation that authorises them, and nothing checks. The bound then *looks* principled while being wrong.

**Target user.** Any team shipping agentic payments on Indian rails; and, pointedly, Razorpay's own protocol team.

**Why it matters (corpus).** This is the **B5 exhibit**, and it is Razorpay's own. `THE_LEGAL_SPINE.md:61-67`: Razorpay's ACP SEP #216 states *"**Why ₹15,000 Limit?** NPCI's UPI Circle specification imposes a hard **per-transaction** limit."* Per NPCI **OC No.201/2024-25, 13 Aug 2024, §7** — *"a maximum monthly limit of ₹15,000/- per delegation and maximum per transaction limit of ₹5000"* — and OC-201B/2025-26 §7 for the IoT/software/AI profile: **₹15,000 is monthly; per-transaction is ₹5,000. Wrong by 3×.** The same SEP contradicts itself two tables later (*"NPCI enforces per-transaction **and monthly** limits"*). It was reviewed by Stripe and Meta and sat open from 2026-04-12, last touched 2026-05-15 (`01_razorpay_signals/razorpay_acp_stalled_upi.md`). Meanwhile MediaNama's coverage of Pine Labs P3P (2026-06-12) names **RBI's ₹15,000 AFA threshold as the unaddressed gap** in a competing Indian agentic protocol (`track01_opportunity.md` E28). The whole Indian agentic space is transacting on a misquoted number.

**Solution.** A `bounds.yaml` where every limit is `{value, unit, scope, source_doc, source_clause, retrieved, checksum}`. A **conformance runner** re-reads the cited primary source, extracts the governing sentence, and asserts the code's value and *scope* (per-transaction vs monthly vs per-delegation vs first-24h) match. Mismatch → build fails. The gate then enforces those bounds at authorisation time with distinct refusal codes per clause.

**AI role.** Load-bearing and hard to replace: **clause extraction and scope classification from regulatory PDFs.** Given OC-201 §7's single sentence containing *two* limits with different scopes, the model must return structured `{₹5,000, per_transaction}` and `{₹15,000, per_month, per_delegation}` — the exact discrimination Razorpay's own engineers got wrong. This is text→structure over adversarial legal prose; regex cannot do it, and we prove that by shipping a regex baseline that fails.
**Where deliberately NOT AI.** The comparison, the assertion, and the runtime gate are deterministic. The LLM never decides whether a payment is allowed — it only proposes what the clause says, and a human-reviewed `APPROVED` checksum must be present before a bound is usable. **Cost:** a newly published circular cannot auto-apply; there is a human in the loop and a stale-bound window, which we measure and report.

**Razorpay role.** UPI Circle / Reserve Pay bounds, UPI Autopay AFA thresholds (₹15,000 general; ₹1,00,000 for MCC 6211/6300/7322/6529/5960 — `track01_opportunity.md` §704), Razorpay test-mode order creation under those bounds. Directly addresses a defect in Razorpay's own open-source contribution.

**Key workflow.** fetch circular → extract clauses → human approve → bounds.yaml → CI conformance → runtime gate → refusal code cites clause → audit trail.

**The batch metric.** **Clause-extraction accuracy over 60+ hand-labelled limit statements** drawn from real NPCI/RBI circulars (OC-201, OC-201B, RBI e-mandate framework RBI/DPSS/2026-27/396, RBI Authentication Directions 2025, the ₹100/day reversal circular DPSS.CO.PD No.629/02.01.014/2019-20), scored on **exact match of (value, unit, scope) jointly** — not value alone, because value-alone is exactly the metric under which SEP #216 would have *passed*. Report per-scope confusion matrix and a **regex baseline**.

**Defence.** B1: the labels are hand-made by us from primary PDFs and the *features* are raw text — there is no derived feature to leak; we additionally publish inter-pass agreement by re-labelling a 20% sample blind. B2: three baselines — first-number-in-sentence, regex for `₹[\d,]+`, and last-number-before-a-scope-word. The naive "first ₹ figure" baseline **produces the SEP #216 error**, which is a devastating table row. B3: ablate the scope classifier and re-report; ablate retrieval and report. B4: attrition ledger of clauses the extractor refused to parse — **refusals are counted in the denominator**, and abstention is reported as its own bucket, never silently dropped. B5: this **is** the B5 idea.

**Product-not-tool.** The product is the authorisation gate. The conformance runner is what makes its bounds trustworthy.

**Technical depth.** PDF clause segmentation, scope taxonomy, checksum-pinned source snapshots, build-failing conformance, refusal-code enforcement at the money boundary.

**Demo.** Point it at Razorpay's own SEP #216. Watch it fail the build and print the OC-201 §7 sentence with the two limits highlighted. That is a 20-second demo moment nobody else in the field can produce.

**Differentiation.** `aryanpajnee/RazorpayBuildathon` occupies the mandate/signature layer competently and *has no measurement at all* (`ARYAN_direct_competitor.md`). This idea does not contest the mandate layer; it contests whether the numbers inside it are true.

**Competitors.** `aryanpajnee` (adjacent, not overlapping); `SweetieBirdX/Kinora` (natural-language licensing terms → machine-enforced gates — closest prior art, from `REPO_SHORTLIST.md` #26).

**Risk to flag.** Requires primary-source PDFs to remain fetchable; `npci.org.in` returned **HTTP 403 to all programmatic access** during research (`market_problems.md` integrity notes). Snapshot everything locally, day one.

**Track.** Primary **T01** · Secondary T05.

## 06 · SHELFCHECK — agent-readability of a merchant catalog, measured with a target that is not a mock

**Pitch.** A benchmark-and-remediation product that measures whether a real AI buyer agent can actually see, choose and buy from a merchant's catalog — with a defensible target, a stated effective n, and a real-model denominator.

**Problem.** Merchants are about to be graded by buying agents and have no idea how they score. The only serious entrant measuring this reports a deterministic mock.

**Target user.** D2C merchant on Razorpay preparing for agentic traffic.

**Why it matters (corpus).** `FIELD_BAR.md` §2 Opening 3 (marked ⭐ strategic): T01 holds **16 of 261 repos (6%)** — thinnest by ~4× — and the sell-side cluster has **exactly one serious occupant**, `Adarsh-Me/Agent-Audit`, *"and its committed evidence is a deterministic mock whose rerun delta is exactly zero."* Its live run was **640 attempted / 234 parse_ok (36.6%)**, disclosed only in `Docs/SUMMARY.md:115`. We additionally check that success is not trivially predicted by product-name length or price with a stump. B2: baselines are (a) raw catalog with no remediation, (b) a naive "add more keywords" remediation, tuned. B3: ablate each remediation category and re-report. B4: this is a headline B4 defence — attrition is on the front page. B5: any spend cap in the demo cites NPCI OC-201B (₹5,000/txn, ₹15,000/month) with the scope stated correctly, explicitly contra SEP #216.

**Product-not-tool.** It changes the merchant's catalog and re-measures. That is a product with a before and an after.

**Technical depth.** Multi-model orchestration with cost control, deterministic scorer, cluster bootstrap, failure taxonomy, refusal codes at the money boundary.

**Demo.** "Your catalog. Three different buying agents. 42% succeed. Here's why. Here's the fix. Now 78%." Then: "and here is the number of trials we threw away."

**Problem.** "Bounded and gated" is asserted everywhere and measured nowhere. Nobody reports the cost of a refusal, so nobody can tell a cautious agent from a broken one.

**Why it matters (corpus).** The T01 bar demands *"Every money action explainable, bounded and gated"* and the field responds with assertions. `VeerGetGit/RazorPay_agentic_checkout` claims Guardrails AI + Llama Prompt Guard while `input_guard.py:14-18` is **three substring scans and zero models**, and `payment_node.py:91` prints *"Payment successful! 🎉"* for an unpaid Order whose own committed DB shows **5 orders, all `status='pending'`** (`FIELD_BAR.md` §2 Opening 4, `INDEX.md`). **Key workflow.** instruction → plan → gate evaluates 6 checks → act or refuse with a coded reason → outcome → nightly refusal-quality report → autonomy tier adjusted.

**The batch metric.** **Refusal precision/recall over 200+ adversarially-constructed cases**, with an explicit **over-refusal cost in ₹** and **the always-refuse baseline reported as a row** (which achieves perfect safety and zero value — the row that makes the metric honest). Autonomy is granted per category only when the **Wilson CI lower bound** clears the gate, with a distinct-case floor to stop correlated reruns inflating n (patterns from `tfthushaar calibration/wilson.py:12-21` and `calibrator.py:40-52`).

**Product-not-tool.** A spending agent that actually spends, under an autonomy budget it earns.

**Technical depth.** Wilson lower bound with anti-gaming guards, reachability testing, threshold-parity build test, cost-priced refusals.

**Demo.** Show the agent buying. Then show it refusing — and show the ₹ cost of that refusal on the same screen. Then show the always-refuse baseline scoring 100% safe and ₹0 delivered.

B4: attrition ledger of orders dropped for missing fields, per-split. B5: n/a.

**Product-not-tool.** It blocks and abstains on live orders.

**Technical depth.** Calibration with proper scoring rules, cost-sensitive thresholding with a human-review cost term, time-based splitting.

**Demo.** Slide the cost ratio. Watch the optimal policy collapse to "never flag" at a plausible ratio, and say that out loud.

## 09 · REPLAY — every LLM sentence about money must survive an arithmetic re-execution

**Pitch.** A settlement Q&A agent where each generated explanation is decomposed into checkable arithmetic claims and re-executed against the source ledger; unverifiable sentences are deleted, not shipped.

**Problem.** Finance agents produce fluent, wrong explanations. A merchant cannot tell which sentences are load-bearing and which are decoration.

**Target user.** Merchant finance analyst asking "why was Tuesday's settlement ₹31,402 short?"

**Why it matters (corpus).** T04's why-now is literally the verification thesis (*"verification capacity, not generation speed, is the bottleneck"*). The field's exemplar of the inverse failure: `Sivanandini`'s *"LLM investigation note"* is **an f-string** (`FIELD_BAR.md` §4), and `abhinav-phi`'s eval LLM **was a noop until Amendment 2** — the author discovered his own evaluator wasn't running (`FIELD_BAR.md` §2 Opening 7). `MrBurber`'s validate-or-discard guardrail (throw unsafe output away rather than retry, DI-testable with no key, author ships his own bypass as a test) is the best pattern in the corpus for this and is rated 9 in `INDEX.md`.

**Solution.** LLM answers in a constrained grammar where every numeric assertion carries `{value, operation, source_row_ids}`. A deterministic replayer re-computes each claim from the ledger. Claims that fail are struck through and a `VERIFIED n/m` badge is attached. Answers below a verification floor are refused entirely.

**AI role.** Load-bearing: mapping a natural-language question to a set of candidate arithmetic decompositions over an unjoined multi-source ledger. Deterministic query planning cannot do this because the join key does not exist (`market_problems.md` #6).
**Where deliberately NOT AI.** The replayer, the ledger, the verification floor. **Cost:** genuinely correct qualitative statements ("this looks like a bank holiday effect") carry no arithmetic and are therefore always struck; we lose real insight and we show an example of what we lost.

**Razorpay role.** Settlement reports, Route splits, MDR/fee lines, refunds — Razorpay's own data shapes.

**Key workflow.** question → retrieve → decompose → generate → replay each claim → strike/keep → verification badge → refuse below floor.

**The batch metric.** **Claim-level verification rate and answer-level accuracy over 150+ questions** with hand-labelled correct answers, reported as `questions_asked → answers_produced → claims_emitted → claims_verified`, plus **hallucination rate = claims struck / claims emitted**, and the **refusal rate** as a first-class number rather than an omission.

**Defence.** B1: questions are authored against ledger facts, and we test that no question's answer is predictable from its surface form. B2: baselines are (a) raw LLM with no replayer, (b) a template SQL answerer that handles only the 5 most common question shapes — the template baseline will be *strong*, and if it is, that is the finding. B3: ablate the replayer and re-report accuracy — this quantifies exactly what verification buys. B4: refusals and parse failures are in the denominator. B5: fee/TDS/TCS constants carry statutory citations in-line.

**Product-not-tool.** An analyst asks questions and gets answers they can act on.

**Technical depth.** Constrained decoding, claim decomposition grammar, deterministic replay, verification floor as a gate.

**Demo.** Ask a question. Watch two of six sentences get struck live, with the failing arithmetic shown.

**Differentiation.** `ch24btech11028-create/recoagent` already advertises *"every explanation must survive an arithmetic replay"* — **this idea is directly occupied by a named rival.** The differentiator would have to be the ablation quantifying what the replayer buys, which that rival almost certainly lacks.

**Competitors.** `ch24btech11028-create/recoagent` (direct), `adityasingh1786/certus-ai-finance-controller` ("Double-Lock"), Razorpay **Settlement Insights** (ships).

**Track.** Primary **T04** · Secondary T03.

**Key workflow.** freeze golden set → change detected → replay → agreement + flip diff → tier adjust → audit entry.

**The batch metric.** **Decision agreement rate across 200+ golden cases, per model version, with a Wilson CI and a distinct-case floor**; plus **flip asymmetry** (cases that went permissive→restrictive vs restrictive→permissive), because a permissive flip is a money event and a restrictive flip is a friction event and they must not be averaged.

B2: baselines are "always the previous decision" and "always the majority decision." B3: ablate the provider-binding guard and demonstrate the mock inheriting autonomy — reproducing `tfthushaar`'s bug on purpose as a test. B4: cases that error out are a named bucket, not a silent drop; the distinct-case floor stops rerun-inflated n. B5: tier ceilings cite OC-201B limits with correct scope.

**Product-not-tool.** It is the safety mechanism of a live agent, not a CI script.

**Technical depth.** Provider-bound trust records, Wilson lower bound with anti-gaming guards, flip-asymmetry accounting.

**Demo.** Swap the model mid-demo. Watch the agent's spend ceiling drop by itself and print the three cases that flipped permissive.

**Differentiation.** Nobody in the census re-measures after a model change. It is also an unusually good source of a *genuine* failure narrative (`THE_REAL_RUBRIC.md` §3 — *"the last one is the one we read first"*).

**Competitors.** None observed in the field. Adjacent: `MukundaKatta/crusoe-nemotron-harness` (`REPO_SHORTLIST.md` #11) — cost/failure/budget harness, no behavioural regression.

## 13 · TAT WATCHDOG — auditing a compensation the regulator already priced

**Pitch.** An agent that detects failed-transaction reversals breaching RBI's turn-around-time windows and computes the ₹100/day compensation the bank owes *suo moto*, with a defensible false-positive rate.

**Problem.** RBI requires compensation for late reversals without a customer complaint. Nothing outside the bank can audit whether it happened.

**Target user.** PSP/merchant ops; ultimately the customer.

**Why it matters (corpus).** `market_problems.md` #16, grade **A**: RBI/2019-20/67, DPSS.CO.PD No.629/02.01.014/2019-20, 20 Sep 2019 — **₹100/day beyond T+1** (UPI P2P, IMPS, NACH, PPI) **or T+5** (ATM, PoS/CNP, UPI merchant), and verbatim *"Wherever financial compensation is involved, the same shall be effected to the customer's account **suo moto, without waiting for a complaint**."* The problem is that suo-moto compensation *"is hard to audit from outside."* This is a rare case where **the label is set by a regulator, not by us** — which is the cleanest possible B1 defence in the entire pool.

**Solution.** Ingest a reversal stream, classify each event's rail and category (which determines T+1 vs T+5), compute the breach and the accrued compensation, and produce a claim pack. Bounded: it files nothing automatically; it produces evidence.

**AI role.** Rail/category classification from messy narration strings and mixed-format statements — the same text→structure job as idea 05, on a different corpus.

**Razorpay role.** Refund/reversal objects and settlement timing in test mode.

**Key workflow.** ingest → classify rail → compute window → breach detection → compensation → claim pack → audit trail.

**The batch metric.** **Breach-detection precision/recall over 300+ synthetic reversal events with injected delays**, where the ground truth is computed from the *circular's own rule*, not from a model — plus **rail-classification accuracy measured separately** so the two error sources are not entangled (this separation is itself a defence: entangling them is how `abhinav-phi` got a diagonal confusion matrix).

**Defence.** B1: the breach label is a deterministic function of the regulator's rule applied to timestamps, and the classifier does not see the timestamps — an architectural separation, not a promise. B2: "flag everything late by any amount" and "flag nothing." B3: ablate the classifier (use a uniform T+5) and re-report. B4: unclassifiable narrations are an abstention bucket in the denominator. B5: this idea is B5-native — every constant cites DPSS.CO.PD No.629.

**Product-not-tool.** It produces a claim pack with money in it.

## 14 · MANDATE DIFF — adjudicating an agent dispute as a document comparison

**Pitch.** A merchant-side dispute engine that, for agent-initiated orders, adjudicates by structurally diffing the signed mandate against the executed order — and reports how often that diff is decisive.

**Problem.** When an AI agent buys the wrong thing, nobody knows who is liable, and the merchant absorbs it by default.

**Target user.** Merchant accepting agent traffic.

**Why it matters (corpus).** `market_problems.md` #12, grade **A**: Google's AP2 announcement (17 Sep 2025, 60+ partners) names the three open problems verbatim — proving the user gave *specific* authority; the merchant knowing the request reflects *true intent*; determining *accountability* when it goes wrong — and answers with signed **Intent** and **Cart** Mandates. The corpus notes *"nothing exists on the merchant side to accept, validate, enforce or retain a mandate"* and that this makes dispute resolution *"document comparison over structured mandates — tractable, and unbuilt."* Compounding it, `market_problems.md` #13: **every fraud heuristic fires the wrong way on a legitimate agent** — data-centre IP, no browsing session, no behavioural history break AVS/device/session signals *simultaneously*. And #10 (grade A, Visa VAMP fact sheet): the ratio is **count-based**, acquirer Excessive ≥70bps, merchant Excessive dropping to **≥150bps on 1 Apr 2026** — so agent disputes are a *compliance-threshold* event, not just refunds. Also relevant: `aryanpajnee`'s correct and non-obvious observation that **"a valid signature proves origin, not permission"** (`ARYAN_direct_competitor.md`).

**Solution.** Retain the mandate with the order. On dispute, produce a structured diff: item, variant, quantity, price, currency, expiry, cap, delegate identity, and a verdict — `WITHIN_MANDATE`, `OUTSIDE_MANDATE`, or `UNDETERMINED`. `UNDETERMINED` is a first-class, counted outcome.

**AI role.** Mapping free-text dispute reasons and merchant evidence onto mandate fields — the messy end. The diff itself is not AI.
**Where deliberately NOT AI.** Signature verification, field comparison, verdict. **Cost:** we cannot adjudicate intent disputes with no mandate field to anchor on; those are always `UNDETERMINED`, and `UNDETERMINED` rate is our most important honest number.

**Razorpay role.** Merchant-side mandate retention alongside Razorpay orders; test-mode dispute simulation.

**Key workflow.** agent order + mandate → retain → dispute → diff → verdict → evidence pack → VAMP-count impact.

**The batch metric.** **Verdict accuracy over 200+ constructed disputes** spanning six mandate-violation archetypes, reported as **accuracy on determined cases *and* the `UNDETERMINED` rate side by side** — because a system that determines 20% of cases perfectly is worse than one that determines 90% at 95%, and reporting only accuracy hides that.

**Defence.** B1: verdicts are a deterministic function of mandate fields; the archetype is planted, so we are validating the instrument. We publish a stump check that violation type is not readable from surface metadata. B2: "always merchant wins", "always buyer wins", and a keyword-matching baseline. B3: ablate each mandate field. B4: `UNDETERMINED` is the denominator discipline. B5: mandate caps cite AP2 schema and, for the Indian rail, OC-201B with the per-transaction/monthly distinction stated correctly.

**Product-not-tool.** It produces dispute evidence packs that a merchant submits.

**Technical depth.** Canonical JSON, Ed25519 verification, structural diff, VAMP-count arithmetic.

**Demo.** Agent buys the 500ml, mandate said 1L. Diff. Verdict. Thirty seconds.

**Differentiation.** The mandate layer itself is occupied by `aryanpajnee` competently; the *dispute* layer is not.

**Competitors.** `aryanpajnee`, `jaybuidl/ask-trivium-hackathon` (multi-LLM adjudication panel — `REPO_SHORTLIST.md` #9), `BackTrackCo/x402r-sdk` (refunds/disputes as primitives, #6).

**Track.** Primary **T01** · Secondary T02.

## 16 · TIMING-NOT-EVASION — a GST mismatch classifier whose label is externally verifiable

**Pitch.** A GST input-tax-credit reconciliation agent that separates "your supplier filed late" from "this invoice does not exist," where the distinction is checkable against filing dates rather than asserted.

**Problem.** GSTR-3B vs GSTR-2B mismatches auto-generate Form DRC-01C within days, algorithmically — and most mismatches are supplier *timing*, not evasion. The taxpayer cannot fix it by being compliant.

**Target user.** Indian SME finance/tax person.

**Why it matters (corpus).** `market_problems.md` #7: mechanism documented (grade **B** for structure), *"excess beyond tolerance auto-generates Form DRC-01C, algorithmically, within days"*; most mismatches are supplier timing (GSTR-1 filed after the 11th / QRMP after the 13th). **Magnitude: `EVIDENCE NOT FOUND` — GSTN/CBIC publish no DRC-01C volume.** That absence is itself usable: we lead with mechanism, not a fabricated number, which is exactly the discipline `market_problems.md` prescribes.

**Solution.** Match invoices across GSTR-1/2B/3B, and classify each mismatch into `timing / amount / missing / duplicate / place-of-supply`, with a projected resolution date for timing cases derived from the supplier's filing cadence.

**AI role.** Fuzzy invoice-identity resolution across records with inconsistent invoice-number formatting — the classic no-join-key problem.
**Where deliberately NOT AI.** Filing-date arithmetic, tolerance rules, DRC-01C threshold. **Cost:** the matcher will refuse near-duplicates a human would merge; refusal rate is published.

**Razorpay role.** Weak — this is adjacent to Razorpay's surface. Honest flag: **this idea scores badly on Razorpay alignment** (`track_scorecard.md` weights it /20).

**Key workflow.** ingest triplet → match → classify → project resolution → exception pack.

**The batch metric.** **Classification accuracy over 400+ synthetic mismatch cases with planted causes**, plus **timing-projection error in days** — and critically, the label for "timing" is verifiable from filing timestamps in the data, not from the generator's intent flag, which is the B1 defence.

**Defence.** B1: label derived from an independently-observable field (filing date), and the classifier is denied access to the generator's cause flag by schema. B2: "always timing" (likely a strong baseline — if it wins, that is the headline). B3: ablate the cadence model. B4: unmatched invoices ledger. B5: the 11th/13th filing deadlines and DRC-01C tolerance carry CBIC citations inline.

**Product-not-tool.** It produces the reply pack for a DRC-01C notice.

**Demo.** 400 mismatches; 340 are "your supplier files on the 14th"; here are the 12 that are real.

**Competitors.** `Samyak17Jain/reconciliation-sentinel` (3-way incl. GST), `SuryaSK-dev` (recon + tax validation).

**Track.** Primary **T04** · Secondary T05.

## 17 · SPENDPROOF — deliberately too ambitious

**Pitch.** A full UPI-Circle-shaped delegated-spend simulator with a conformance oracle, an adversarial mandate-attack suite, a formal state machine, and a differential test against Razorpay's own ACP handler.

**Problem.** There is no way to test whether an agentic spend implementation actually obeys Indian rails, because the rail is a CUG pilot and the procedural guidelines are members-only.

**Why it matters (corpus).** `india_rails_delegated_payments.md`: *"An AI agent in India today can legally be authorised to spend at most ₹5,000 per transaction and ₹15,000 per month, from a single delegating user's bank account, on domestic P2M transactions only — and only as one of at most 5 delegated profiles, inside NPCI's Closed User Group pilot, with every debit requiring 'explicit user action'"* (OC-201B, 8 Oct 2025). And: *"Detailed revocation TAT / API — `EVIDENCE NOT FOUND`; the Procedural Guidelines are **members-only and not public**."* Plus the first-24-hour limit is **contradictory between the circulars (₹5,000) and NPCI's own product page (₹2,000)** — an unresolved source conflict the corpus flags explicitly.

**Solution.** A reference simulator + conformance suite: 5-delegate cap, per-transaction, monthly envelope, first-24h window (tested under *both* the ₹2,000 and ₹5,000 readings), P2M-only, explicit-user-action requirement, revocation semantics, mandate lifetime 1 month–5 years. Differentially tested against Razorpay's ACP SEP #216 handler behaviour.

**The batch metric.** **Conformance pass rate over 150+ generated spend scenarios** including boundary and adversarial cases, with a per-rule breakdown and an explicit `UNSPECIFIED` bucket for behaviour the public documents do not determine — **the size of the `UNSPECIFIED` bucket is the most interesting finding**, because it quantifies how much of India's agentic rail is publicly untestable.

**Why it is in the pool as "too ambitious."** It requires the protocol layer *and* the simulator *and* the differential harness in ~8 days, and its most valuable output (the `UNSPECIFIED` census) is a research finding rather than a merchant-facing product — it strains the T05/T01 "working product" bar. But its core artifact — *"here is what the public record does not determine, and here is where Razorpay's own SEP got it wrong"* — may be the single most distinctive thing available in this competition.

**Track.** Primary **T01** · Secondary T05.

## 18 · CE3.0 QUALIFIER — evidence assembly with an honest denominator

**Pitch.** A chargeback-evidence responder that reports what fraction of disputes it could qualify under Visa CE3.0 — including the ones it could not, and why.

**Problem.** Merchants fight representments after the fact and lose. The leverage is upstream, at evidence assembly.

**Why it matters (corpus).** `market_problems.md` #10, grade **A**, Visa VAMP fact sheet 2025 verbatim: ratio = count[Fraud TC40 + Disputes TC15] ÷ settled TC05; acquirer Above Standard ≥50bps, Excessive ≥70bps; merchant Excessive **≥220bps → ≥150bps on 1 Apr 2026**; effective 1 Jun 2025. And the leverage point: **the ratio excludes CE3.0-qualified fraud and pre-dispute-resolved disputes.** Footnote, verbatim: *"Programs for Brazil, Chile, and India will be announced later"* — so India's parameters are unannounced, which must be stated rather than assumed (a B5 discipline). The task is *"assembling artefacts (order, device, AVS, delivery, prior-transaction history) and judging whether they meet CE3.0 matching criteria."*

**Solution.** Ingest disputes, assemble evidence, evaluate CE3.0 matching criteria (two qualifying prior transactions 120–365 days old sharing two of: device ID, IP, delivery address, account ID), and emit either a qualified representment pack or a coded refusal.

**AI role.** Narrative construction for the representment; evidence-gap explanation. Not the qualification decision.
**Where deliberately NOT AI.** CE3.0 criteria evaluation is a rule and must be, because it is a *published network rule* — using a model here would be strictly worse and slower, and saying so is a direct pillar-3 hit. **Cost:** none we can identify, which we state.

**The batch metric.** **CE3.0 qualification rate over 300+ synthetic disputes with planted evidence configurations**, reported with the **non-qualification reason distribution** — and separately, **criteria-evaluation accuracy against hand-computed ground truth**, so the rule engine's correctness and the population's qualifiability are not conflated.

**Defence.** B1: qualification is a deterministic function of published criteria; label is not learned. B2: "submit everything" and "submit nothing" baselines with their VAMP-count consequences. B3: ablate each evidence source. B4: disputes with incomplete evidence are a named bucket. B5: India's VAMP parameters are **unannounced** and the system says so rather than assuming AP/Canada/EU/US thresholds apply — the exact discipline SEP #216 lacked.

**Competitors.** Razorpay **Dispute Responder** ships (Agent Studio collision). Field: `srijan2607/fraud-pulse`.

**Track.** Primary **T02** · Secondary T03.

## 19 · MSME CLOCK — a receivables chaser whose escalation ladder is statutory

**Pitch.** An invoice-chasing agent whose escalation timing is derived from Section 43B(h) and the MSMED 45-day rule rather than from a made-up cadence, with a measured promise-to-pay conversion rate.

**Problem.** ₹7.34 lakh crore of MSME receivables are frozen and the statutory remedy behaves like litigation, not cash flow.

**Why it matters (corpus).** `market_problems.md` #2 (grade **B**, with the publishers' advocacy affiliation flagged): **₹7.34 lakh crore as of March 2024**, down from ₹10.7 lakh crore in 2022 — *Delayed Payments Report 3.0*, GAME + FISME + C2FO. MSME Samadhaan has disposed **~19.6% of applications and ~14.7% of disputed value**. The incentive changed on **1 Apr 2024**: Section 43B(h) means late payment to a Udyam-registered micro/small supplier **disallows the buyer's tax deduction** — so the leverage is a tax consequence with a date, not a phone call.

**Solution.** Ledger → Udyam status resolution → day-45 clock → tiered outreach whose escalation *cites the statutory consequence at the right moment* → promise-to-pay tracking → honest conversion reporting.

**AI role.** Message generation and objection handling in the debtor's language; extraction of Udyam/GSTIN identity from messy invoice documents.
**Where deliberately NOT AI.** The 45-day arithmetic, escalation gating, and every statutory claim in an outgoing message, which is drawn from a fixed, cited template library — a model must never improvise a legal assertion. **Cost:** less persuasive, less personalised escalation copy; measured against an LLM-freeform arm in a *shadow* mode that never sends.

**The batch metric.** **Promise-to-pay conversion and days-to-payment over 500+ synthetic invoices, differenced against a never-contacted holdout**, with an attrition ledger and **a separate, hard-gated count of outgoing messages containing an uncited legal claim, which must be zero or the build fails.**

**Defence.** B1: payment outcome is an observed event. B2: holdout + fixed-cadence chaser + tuned. B3: ablate the statutory-citation tier — does citing 43B(h) actually move conversion? If not, publish that; it is a genuinely interesting null. B4: opt-outs and unreachable contacts ledgered (contra `shubhambhattog`'s hardcoded `0`). B5: 43B(h), MSMED §15, and the day-45 rule cited inline.

**Competitors.** `Chinmay0608/razorpay-buildathon-receivables-chaser`; ~63 T03 repos.

**Track.** Primary **T03** · Secondary T05.

## Cross-cutting observations for the scoring pass

1. **Track transfer is real and asymmetric.** The measurement-centric framing is *strongest in T01*, because T01 is the only track where the field has no measurement at all yet (`ARYAN_direct_competitor.md`: the direct competitor has 153 unit tests and **no batch metric, no held-out set, no baseline, no ablation**), and *weakest in T04*, where nine named rivals are already advertising adversarial self-audit, arithmetic replay and eval harnesses (`REPO_SHORTLIST.md` rival table). **T02 is the track whose own bar most explicitly rewards this framing** (*"honest metrics including false-positive cost"*, *"held-out test set"*) — but it also carries the Agent Studio collision and the only explicit disqualifier.
2. **The single most reusable artifact across the whole pool is idea 01's adversarial baseline hunt.** It can be embedded in any of 02, 03, 04, 06, 08, 10, 16 as a ~30-line component and a README table.
3. **The highest-leverage *narrative* asset is a real, unchosen failure** (`THE_REAL_RUBRIC.md` §3). Ideas 05, 11 and 17 are the most likely to generate one organically, because each involves discovering that something you believed was true — a cited limit, an inherited autonomy tier, an unspecified rule — was not.
4. **Every idea here must survive the "is the AI decorative?" test.** The genuinely load-bearing AI roles in this pool are: clause extraction with scope (05, 13), decline-string→cause mapping (10), catalog interpretation by a real buyer model (06), fuzzy identity resolution without a join key (04, 16), and instruction-ambiguity judgement (07). The advisory-narrative roles (03, 08, 12, 19) are **weak on rubric pillar 3** and should be scored down accordingly unless the non-use argument is made to carry the weight instead.
